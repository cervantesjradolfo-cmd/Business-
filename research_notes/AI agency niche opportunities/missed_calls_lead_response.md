# Missed Calls, Missed Leads and Slow Lead Response: US Local Service Businesses by Industry

Notes on sources: nearly all missed-call data comes from call-tracking, answering-service or AI-receptionist vendors, which have an incentive to show high miss rates. This is flagged as [VENDOR] below. "Missed", "unanswered", "abandoned" and "failed to connect" are defined differently from one vendor to the next, so rates are not directly comparable across studies. I could not open some primary reports (Invoca, CallRail, Clio and 411 Locals are gated or behind forms), so several figures come from press releases or secondary coverage, and this is noted where it applies.

## Q1. What share of inbound calls go unanswered, by industry?

### Takeaway
Across studies, local businesses miss roughly 10–40% of inbound calls. In small-business secret-shopper style samples the rate goes above 60%. The worst-documented verticals are healthcare/dental (about 20–38%), law firms (28% to roughly 50–60% on first call), salons/spas (30–37%) and home services. Home services numbers vary widely by definition, from 14% to 48% unanswered. Auto service (about 10–21%) and real estate (about 9%, CallRail) look better.

### Cited Findings
**Cross-industry**
- **411 Locals (2024) [VENDOR, local marketing firm]:** monitored inbound calls at 85 small businesses in 58 industries for 30 days. Only 37.8% of calls were answered by a live person, so 62.2% went unanswered. One summary splits this into 37.8% to voicemail and 24.3% with no answer and no voicemail. I could not reach the primary report; figures come via secondary summaries — [Alliance Virtual Offices summary](https://www.alliancevirtualoffices.com/virtual-office-blog/shocking-research-finds-small-businesses-miss-almost-half-of-incoming-calls/); [Aira summary](https://getaira.io/blog/missed-business-calls-statistics)
- **CallRail small-business benchmark (Jan 2025) [VENDOR, call tracking]:** based on 1.1M leads. Missed-call rate was 32% in healthcare (the highest), 28% in legal, 14% in home services and 9% in real estate. CallRail treats calls under 15 seconds as "abandoned" rather than "missed", which lowers its rates. Automotive was in the dataset, but no rate for it was surfaced — [BusinessWire press release, Jan 14 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250114793850/en/CallRail-Releases-Report-Benchmarking-Marketing-Efforts-for-Small-Businesses)
- Secondary coverage attributes this claim to CallRail: up to 85% of callers whose calls go unanswered will not call back. I did not verify it in the primary release — [Quo blog](https://www.quo.com/blog/small-business-callback-statistics/)

**Home services / trades (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, contractors)**
- **Invoca Home Services Lead Conversion Benchmarks Report 2026 (July 2026) [VENDOR, call analytics]:** covers 9 home-services sub-industries. A person answered 52% of all inbound calls. The answer rate was 65% for calls over 15 seconds and 73% for calls over 30 seconds. 38% of digital-marketing calls were leads, 45% of those converted on the call, and 55% of businesses never asked the lead to buy or book. The report is gated, so I worked from the landing page and summaries — [Invoca report page](https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026); [PPC Land coverage](https://ppc.land/invoca-packages-ai-agents-as-google-bills-missed-lsa-calls-over-20-seconds/)
- **Same PPC Land article:** Invoca packaged AI agents as Google began billing for missed Local Services Ads calls over 20 seconds. That means trades now pay for leads they don't answer — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/invoca-packages-ai-agents-as-google-bills-missed-lsa-calls-over-20-seconds/)
- **CallRail (Jan 2025):** home services missed 14% (see above). The gap from Invoca's 48% unanswered comes from different datasets and definitions, such as the 15-second cutoff and whether spam, vendor or repeat calls are counted — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250114793850/en/CallRail-Releases-Report-Benchmarking-Marketing-Efforts-for-Small-Businesses)
- **Low-credibility vendor analyses:** one reports 74.1% of 130,175 calls from 45 contractors going unanswered, with HVAC at 70% and plumbing at 66.7%. The methodology is unpublished, so treat it as low-credibility [VENDOR] — [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/home-services-missed-call-revenue-statistics-2026); [ainora.lt](https://ainora.lt/blog/hvac-service-call-statistics-2026)

**Law firms**
- **Clio Legal Trends Report 2024 (Oct 2024) [VENDOR, legal practice software, but the de facto industry standard]:** secret-shopper test of 1,000 firms by email and 500 by phone.
  - 67% of firms did not respond to email (60% in 2019).
  - 48% either didn't answer the phone or didn't call back, roughly 20 points worse than 2019, when 27% didn't answer or return calls.
  - Only 40% answered the initial call (56% in 2019). Including callbacks, 52% were reached.
  - Only 2% of email replies addressed the shopper's situation (27% in 2019).
  - 7% of firms use chatbots, while 61% of clients said they would use a chatbot if they knew a human would eventually follow up.
  - Sources: [Above the Law](https://abovethelaw.com/2024/10/law-firms-failing-at-the-single-most-basic-lawyering-task/); [2Civility / Illinois Supreme Court Commission on Professionalism](https://www.2civility.org/2024-clio-legal-trends-report-fixing-the-first-impression-problem-for-law-firms/); [ABA Law Practice Magazine, Mar–Apr 2025](https://americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/resources/law-practice-magazine/2025/march-april-2025/laws-new-first-impression-transforming-client-intake)
- **CallRail (2025):** legal missed 28% of calls — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250114793850/en/CallRail-Releases-Report-Benchmarking-Marketing-Efforts-for-Small-Businesses)

**Dental / medical**
- **CallRail (2025):** healthcare had the highest miss rate of any industry, at 32% — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250114793850/en/CallRail-Releases-Report-Benchmarking-Marketing-Efforts-for-Small-Businesses)
- **Peerlogic (Feb 2026) [VENDOR, dental phone AI]:** 4,280 calls across 26 practices. 38% went unanswered, and new-patient conversion was 25% — [Peerlogic DSO case study](https://www.peerlogic.com/post/peerlogic-dso-case-study)
- **Patient News data, reported by DentistryIQ [VENDOR]:** the average North American practice misses 20% of new-patient calls. The top 10% of practices answer 95% and convert 75% — [DentistryIQ](https://dentistryiq.com/practice-management/patient-relationships/article/14184456/dentists-stand-to-gain-150000-in-profits-with-phone-call-data)
- **Getreach, as cited by VoiceStack [VENDOR]:** 32% of dental calls go unanswered during business hours, and abandonment reaches 25% or more at lunch — [VoiceStack](https://voicestack.com/resources/article/why-missed-calls-are-killing-your-dental-practice)

**Auto repair / auto service**
- **Marchex 2019 Auto Services Industry Report [VENDOR; older]:** 8.7M+ calls across 11 US auto-services brands. 13 of every 100 calls failed to connect, through hold abandons, voicemail, transfers or no answer — [Marchex 2019 PDF](https://www.marchex.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/2019-Auto-Services-Industry-Report.pdf)
- **Later Marchex analysis of multi-location operators [VENDOR]:** up to 21% of service calls went unanswered. Several major operators were at about 11–14%, against a "healthy" benchmark of about 10% — [Marchex](https://www.marchex.com/up-to-21-percent-of-automotive-service-customer-calls-go-unanswered-creating-significant-revenue-leakage/)
- **CallRevu mid-year 2026 benchmark [VENDOR; dealership service departments]:** 22.4% of promised callbacks were not completed — [Ratchet+Wrench](https://www.ratchetandwrench.com/news/researchreports/article/55396841/callrevu-releases-mid-year-2026-benchmark-report-3-takeaways-for-auto-shop-owners-can-take-to-reduce-hangups-and-increase-revenue)

**Salons / spas**
- **Zenoti [VENDOR, salon software; HyperConnect processes 1M+ calls a month]:**
  - 37% of salon/spa calls go unanswered, and 82% of those misses happen during business hours, not after hours.
  - Other Zenoti pages put the miss rate at 30–35%.
  - 71% of regulars say they have skipped booking because the salon was hard to reach.
  - Source: [Zenoti, AI receptionist ROI](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/ai-receptionist-roi)

**Real estate**
- **CallRail (2025):** 9% of real-estate calls missed — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250114793850/en/CallRail-Releases-Report-Benchmarking-Marketing-Efforts-for-Small-Businesses)
- **Undated secret shop of real-estate listing inquiries:** 46% went unanswered — [Chicago Agent Magazine](https://chicagoagentmagazine.com/?p=14588)

### Inferences
- The most acute and best-documented pain is in law firms (Clio's longitudinal secret shop shows it getting worse from 2019 to 2024), healthcare/dental (highest CallRail rate, with high value per new patient) and salons (high miss rate even during business hours). Home services has the highest dollar value per missed call (see Q3) and documented low answer rates (Invoca 52%).
- Zenoti's finding that 82% of salon misses happen during business hours suggests the core problem is overflow when staff are busy with customers, not only after-hours coverage. That favors an AI receptionist that is always on, not just an after-hours answering service.
- The 411 Locals figure of about 62% unanswered is widely repeated, but it comes from a small sample (85 businesses). Use it as a headline, not a benchmark.

### Gaps
- I could not reach the full primary reports for Invoca 2026, CallRail 2025, Clio 2024 or 411 Locals; figures come from press releases and secondary coverage.
- I found no independent, peer-reviewed missed-call study for any vertical, and no figures for general contractors or remodelers on their own.
- I did not find ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Podium, Ruler, Numa or Ruby missed-call benchmarks with traceable methodology.
- The often-cited "Automotive Service Association: 23% missed" figure could not be verified.

## Q2. What does research show about speed-to-lead, and how many businesses never respond to web forms?

### Takeaway
The classic studies, MIT/InsideSales (2007) and HBR (2011), show steep decay. Contact within 5 minutes beats 30 minutes by 100x on contact odds and 21x on qualification odds, and within 1 hour beats later contact by about 7x. Secret shops consistently find that roughly a quarter to two-thirds of businesses never respond to a web inquiry at all. Both classic studies are 15+ years old and come from InsideSales, a vendor.

### Cited Findings
- **MIT / InsideSales "Lead Response Management" study (presented Oct 16, 2007; Dr. James Oldroyd and Dave Elkington) [VENDOR-affiliated; 19 years old]:**
  - Odds of contacting a lead are 100x greater when first contact comes within 5 minutes rather than 30.
  - Odds of qualifying the lead are 21x greater.
  - The sample is described as 6 companies, 15,000+ leads and 100,000+ dial attempts. Sources disagree on the time span.
  - These multipliers are often wrongly credited to HBR.
  - Sources: [original summary deck (mortech.com host)](https://www.mortech.com/hs-fs/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf); [InsideSales](https://resources.insidesales.com/?p=4167)
- **HBR, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" (Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington; March 2011) [15 years old; co-author from InsideSales]:**
  - An audit of 2,241 US companies found 37% responded to a web test lead within 1 hour, 16% in 1–24 hours, 24% after more than 24 hours, and 23% never responded.
  - A separate analysis of 1.25M leads at 29 B2C and 13 B2B US companies found that firms contacting within 1 hour were about 7x more likely to qualify the lead than those waiting even one hour longer, and over 60x more likely than those waiting 24 hours or more.
  - I confirmed this through the bibliographic record and secondary summaries, not the full HBR text.
  - Sources: [BYU ScholarsArchive record](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9711); [ainora.lt compilation](https://ainora.lt/blog/lead-response-time-statistics-every-study-2026)
- **Drift lead response survey (2017) [VENDOR; B2B; older]:** 433 companies. Only 7% responded within 5 minutes, and 55% had not responded within 5 business days. Secondary sources disagree on whether the sample was "B2B" or "B2B SaaS" — [ainora.lt compilation](https://ainora.lt/blog/lead-response-time-5-minutes-study-2026); [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/speed-to-lead-statistics)
- **Workato (2024) [VENDOR; B2B]:** tested 114 B2B companies and found over 99% failed to respond within 5 minutes — [Workato](https://workato.com/the-connector/lead-response-time-study)
- **Clio Legal Trends 2024:** 67% of 1,000 law firms never replied to an email inquiry — [Above the Law](https://abovethelaw.com/2024/10/law-firms-failing-at-the-single-most-basic-lawyering-task/)
- **Franchise Update Media "Mystery Shopping '24":** web-form leads were submitted to 92 franchise brand websites, and only half followed up. Of those that followed up, 56% called within 8 hours and 33% after more than 24 hours — [Franchising.com](https://www.franchising.com/articles/mystery_shopping_24_researchers_investigate_first_contact_with_potential_fr.html)
- **2024 homebuilder online follow-up secret shop:** 44% of builders responded in under 1 hour, and 72% followed up by phone — [Do You Convert](https://www.doyouconvert.com/blog/27-2024-homebuilder-online-followup-survey-results/)
- **UPCEA secret-shopper analysis (Dec 2025; higher education, not local services):** 44% of inquiries got no response, the average response time was 14h 23m, and the median has risen since 2023 — [UPCEA](https://upcea.edu/secret-shopper-2025/)
- **Invoca 2026:** 55% of home-services businesses never asked the caller to book. This is a failure of follow-through, not only of response — [Invoca](https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026)

### Inferences
- From 2011 (HBR, 23% never respond) to 2024 (Clio, 67% of law firms never reply by email; franchises, 50% no follow-up), there is no evidence the never-respond rate has improved for small businesses.
- The 5-minute window is effectively unattainable for owner-operators who are on a job site or in a chair. This is the core argument for instant automated response by SMS, chat or AI callback.

### Gaps
- I found no recent (2022–2026) speed-to-lead study specific to local service businesses such as trades, dental or salons; the studies cited are B2B, franchise, legal or education.
- The 2007 MIT/InsideSales and 2011 HBR samples are dated and partly vendor-generated. No independent replication was found.

## Q3. After-hours calling, online booking demand, and revenue lost per missed call

### Takeaway
After-hours call share is contested. ServiceTitan platform data shows only about 10–14% of HVAC calls come outside business hours, while vendor blogs claim 30–60%+. Most misses happen during business hours (Zenoti: 82%). Consumer demand for online and self-booking is high, but the figures come from vendor roundups of uneven quality. Per-missed-call value in trades runs from a few hundred dollars for service calls to $4K–$12K for replacement jobs, but these are vendor models, not measured averages.

### Cited Findings
**After-hours calls**
- **ServiceTitan 2025 platform data, as cited by FieldCamp [VENDOR, secondary]:** the share of HVAC calls outside business hours ranged from 9.8% (October) to 14.1% (June) — [FieldCamp](https://fieldcamp.ai/ai-receptionist/after-hours-call-statistics/)
- **Vendor blogs (higher claims):**
  - Home services: 38–42% after hours, or 62% outside 9-to-5.
  - Legal: anywhere from 33% to 60–70%.
  - None of these has a traceable methodology [VENDOR] — [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/blog/after-hours-calls-losing-revenue); [TRTC](https://trtc.io/blog/details/after-hours-call-statistics)
- **Zenoti:** 82% of missed salon/spa calls occur during business hours — [Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/ai-receptionist-roi)

**Online booking demand (all vendor-compiled, with conflicting figures)**
- 67% to 82% of consumers prefer online booking (both attributed to a 2025 GetApp survey; sources conflict), and 40–46% of online bookings happen outside business hours. Zipwhip is cited for 43% — [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/hub/scheduling/online-booking-statistics); [SchedulingKit self-service stats](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/self-service-booking-statistics)

**Revenue lost per missed call**
- **HVAC [VENDOR models]:**
  - QuoteIQ models a $380 average service ticket at 18% conversion, giving about $21.4K a year lost for a 5-tech shop missing 6 calls a week. It says a missed summer AC call is often a $4K–$12K replacement opportunity.
  - Other vendors cite about $400+ per booked ticket and about $4,200 per emergency job.
  - Sources: [QuoteIQ](https://myquoteiq.com/?p=22669); [Twig](https://www.twig.so/blog/hvac-call-answering-ai)
- **Dental [VENDOR]:** lost value per new patient is estimated at about $700–$1,200 (Solutionreach benchmarks, cited secondarily) — [Simbo.ai](https://www.simbo.ai/blog/?p=28017); [DentistryIQ](https://dentistryiq.com/practice-management/patient-relationships/article/14184456/dentists-stand-to-gain-150000-in-profits-with-phone-call-data)
- One source states that no published study puts a measured dollar figure on missed calls for HVAC shops; widely repeated figures such as "$126K/year" are formula outputs — [ainora.lt](https://ainora.lt/blog/hvac-service-call-statistics-2026)

### Inferences
- The strongest pitch is overflow and busy-hours coverage plus after-hours capture, not after-hours alone. Seasonal peaks in trades, such as HVAC calls rising in summer, are when both misses and ticket values spike.
- A defensible ROI model for prospects is: missed calls a month × booking rate × average ticket, using the prospect's own call logs. Industry dollar-per-call figures are not well sourced.

### Gaps
- I found no primary GetApp, Zipwhip or HubSpot after-hours or online-booking reports. The "83% prefer booking outside 9–5 (HubSpot)" claim could not be verified.
- I found no measured, independent average value per missed call in any vertical, and no Clio data on legal-matter value per missed intake call.

## Q4. Front-desk and labor shortages pushing businesses toward automation

### Takeaway
Dentistry has the clearest data: staffing was the number-one challenge cited by 62% of dentists for 2025. In 2021, more than 70% found it extremely or very hard to recruit administrative staff, though by 2024 front-office shortages were milder than clinical ones. Receptionist employment is flat to declining with heavy replacement churn, and construction/trades need about 440–500K new workers a year.

### Cited Findings
- **ADA Health Policy Institute (late-2024 survey, reported Feb 27 2025):**
  - About 62% of dentists named staffing shortages as their biggest challenge for 2025.
  - Among dentists actively recruiting, hiring hygienists was very or extremely challenging for about 90% (95% in late 2022), and hiring assistants for 70%+ (about 87% in late 2022).
  - 30–40% recruited hygienists or assistants in the prior 3 months.
  - A practice owner reports chronic interview no-shows for front-desk roles since 2020.
  - Source: [ADA News, Feb 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/february/five-years-later-staffing-shortages-infection-control-since-the-covid-19-pandemic/)
- **ADA HPI 2021 poll [older]:** more than 70% of dentists found it extremely or very challenging to recruit administrative staff, and 67.1% raised administrative-staff pay — [Becker's Dental](https://www.beckersdental.com/dentists/more-than-80-of-hiring-dentists-experiencing-recruitment-challenges-ada); [ADA News 2021](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2021/august/staffing-challenges-becoming-major-issue-in-dental-offices/)
- **2024 ADA presentation slides:** about 3/4 of dentists say they have enough office/admin staff, versus about 2/3 for hygienists and assistants. Front-desk shortages have eased relative to clinical ones — [UW panel slides, Mar 2024](https://dental.washington.edu/wp-content/media/Edgar_UWash_Panel_Slides_rev031424.pdf)
- **BLS Occupational Outlook, Receptionists:**
  - About 1.0M jobs in 2024, 45% of them in healthcare and social assistance.
  - The 2024–34 projection was little or no change, with about 128,500 openings a year.
  - The current 2025–35 projection is a 2% decline, with about 105,100 openings a year, mostly replacement churn.
  - Median wage was $18.27 an hour in May 2025.
  - Source: [BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/receptionists.htm)
- **Associated Builders and Contractors (Jan 2025):** construction needs about 439,000 net new workers in 2025 (501K in 2024) and about 499K in 2026 — [Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/2025-workers-construction-needs-how-many/738205/)

### Inferences
- Constant receptionist turnover (about 105–128K openings a year on a base of about 1M jobs, mostly replacements) at about $18/hr (roughly $38K a year before overhead) is the cost baseline an AI receptionist competes against.
- In trades, scarce technicians mean owners and techs answer phones from the field, which explains high miss rates. Freeing techs from phones is a labor-leverage argument as much as a revenue argument.
- The BLS projection of declining receptionist employment is consistent with automation substitution, but BLS does not attribute it to AI in the material I saw.

### Gaps
- I found no current (2024–2026) quantified front-desk turnover or vacancy-duration data from ADA, MGMA or AAO. The "45–60 days to fill front desk" figure (ainora.lt) is unsourced and was excluded.
- I found no trade-association data (ACCA, PHCC) on office or CSR staffing shortages specifically, and no BLS turnover rate for receptionists specifically.
