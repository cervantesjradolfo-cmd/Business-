# AI Adoption Rates Among US Small and Local Businesses, by Industry/Sector

Research date: October 2026. Comparing across surveys requires care because definitions differ widely. Here is how the measures range, from strictest to loosest:
- **Census BTOS** (nationally representative, firm-weighted, "used AI in the last two weeks"): about 18-20% (2025-26).
- **JPMorganChase Institute** (paid AI tools in bank transaction data): 17.7% ever-adopted by end of 2025.
- **NFIB** (members, spring 2025): 24%.
- **Fed SBCS** (employer firms, convenience sample, fall 2025): 46%.
- **US Chamber, Thryv and Goldman 10KSB** (self-selected or online panels, which count "includes software that now uses AI"): 55-76%.

The Census and JPMC figures agree closely at about 18%, and both show the same sector ranking.

---

## 1. What do official/large surveys show about AI use by industry?

### Takeaway
The nationally representative data (Census BTOS, Fed analysis, JPMC transaction data) show the same split. Knowledge sectors run at roughly 30-40% current use: Information, Professional/Scientific/Technical, Education, and Finance. Physical/local-service sectors run under 10%: Construction, Accommodation & Food Services, Transportation, Agriculture, and probably Other Services such as salons and auto repair. Vendor and advocacy surveys report much higher absolute levels, but they generally confirm the ordering. One exception is Thryv, which puts restaurant/retail high.

### Cited Findings

**US Census Bureau BTOS (nationally representative; ~1.2M firms sampled biweekly, ~20,000 responses per wave)**
- Revised question, Nov 2025 onward ("AI in any business function"). AI supplement reference period Nov 2025-Jan 2026: 18% of firms used AI (32% employment-weighted); 22% expect to within 6 months. [Census CES-WP-26-25, Apr 2026](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf)
- Top sectors (firm-weighted current use): Information 38%, Professional/Scientific/Technical 34%, Educational Services 31%, Finance & Insurance 30%, Real Estate 24%; multi-sector firms 24%. Expected in 6 months: Information 43%, Professional 37%, Education 35%, Finance 34%. Sectors "relying on physical output, such as Manufacturing, Construction, and Transportation and Warehousing, and trade sectors (Wholesale and Retail), have much lower adoption rates." — [Census CES-WP-26-25](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf)
- As of the May 3, 2026 survey period: national rate 19.8%. Information 39.7% (≈42% expected), Finance & Insurance 33.9% (≈39% expected), Retail Trade ≈14% (≈17% expected). Overall use stayed between 17% and 20% from Dec 2025 to May 2026. — [Census Library story, May 2026](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html)
- Lowest sectors per the Minneapolis Fed reading of BTOS (including the April 2026 AI supplement): Agriculture, Transportation, Accommodation & Food Services, and Construction are each **under 10%**. Health care ≈20%, Manufacturing ≈12%, Information and Professional Services ≈40%. — [Minneapolis Fed, May 29, 2026](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2026/ai-adoption-in-business-grows-steadily-but-unevenly)
- Fed Board note using BTOS at end of 2025: Information 37%, Professional/Scientific/Technical ≈33%, Finance ≈30%, Real Estate 24%, Wholesale 13%, **Accommodation & Food Services 8%**. Worker-reported generative AI use from the Real-Time Population Survey is 70% in Information versus 21% in Accommodation/Food. — [Federal Reserve FEDS Note, Apr 3, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html)
- By size under the new BTOS question: firms with <20 employees ≈18%; firms with 1-4 employees ≈18% current and ≈21% expected. That compares with 25% current / 34% expected for 100-249 employees and 31% / 40% for 250+. The gap between current and expected use is smallest among micro firms. [Census CES-WP-26-25](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf). The Census story says firms with ≤4 employees are below 20%, firms with <20 employees showed no significant change Dec 2025 to May 2026, and 250+ firms reached 37%. [Census, May 2026](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html)
- Lagging states (9-12% current use): ND, WV, AR, LA, MS, AL. Leading states (20-24%): CO, AZ, NV, FL. — [Census CES-WP-26-25](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf)

**JPMorganChase Institute (Chase business-banking transactions; ~4.6M small businesses, 2019-2025; "adopter" = has ever paid for one of 500+ AI tools)**
- Overall adoption: 4.5% (2022), 8.3% (end 2023), 11.6% (end 2024), **17.7% (end 2025)**. Employer firms are at 26.1% and nonemployer firms at 15.3% (Dec 2025). — [JPMC Institute](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/business-growth-and-entrepreneurship/understanding-ai-use-by-small-businesses)
- By industry, end of 2025: Information 39.3%, Professional Services 30.3%, Educational Services 29.5%, **Construction 8.9%**, **Transportation & Warehousing 5.4%** (lowest highlighted). All were near zero to 5% in 2019. Adoption rates for restaurants, retail, health care and Other Services are not published, only spending mixes. — [JPMC Institute](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/business-growth-and-entrepreneurship/understanding-ai-use-by-small-businesses)
- Limitation: free tools and AI features embedded in other software are not counted, so the series understates use. Its 17.7% closely matches the BTOS 17.8%. — same source

**US Chamber of Commerce, "Empowering Small Business" 2025 (3,870 small businesses with <250 employees; fielded June 6-26, 2025)**
- 58% use AI, up from about 40% in 2024 and 23% in 2023. By industry: Technology 77%, Financial services 74%, Entertainment/media 65%, **Construction 47%**, **Manufacturing 46%**. — [IPWatchdog summary of the Chamber report, Aug 18, 2025](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/08/18/us-chamber-report-small-businesses-rapidly-adopting-ai-despite-regulatory-concerns/); [US Chamber report page](https://uschamber.com/technology/empowering-small-business-the-impact-of-technology-on-u-s-small-business)
- Inconsistency: the same summary also says generative AI is used by 44% as a tool category, while other coverage describes 58% as "generative AI" use. — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/08/18/us-chamber-report-small-businesses-rapidly-adopting-ai-despite-regulatory-concerns/)

**Thryv "AI and Small Business Adoption 2025" (540 SMB decision-makers, 1-100 employees, $100K-$9.9M revenue; fielded May 4-14, 2025; vendor survey)**
- 55% use AI ("including software you have been using that now uses AI"), up from 39% in 2024.
- By industry: **Construction 48%**, Professional Services 71%, Restaurant/Retail 61%.
- By size: 1-4 employees 37%, 5-9 employees 53%, 10-100 employees 68%.
- Optimism that AI is a strategic edge: about 56% of blue-collar firms versus 71-72% of services and white-collar firms. — [Thryv PDF](https://assets.thryv.com/prod/media/pdfs/guides/AI-and-Small-Business-Adoption-2025-FINAL.pdf)

**NFIB "Small Business and Technology 2025" (fielded Mar 6-31, 2025; 20,000 members sampled, n=514 responses to the AI question)**
- 24% of small business owners currently use AI tools such as ChatGPT or Canva. By size: nonemployers 18% (n=34), 1-9 employees 21%, 10-19 employees 24%, 20-49 employees 28%, 50+ employees 48% (small n).
- 75% of non-users have no plans to adopt in the next 12 months.
- 42% are "not at all familiar" with AI.
- The sample skews toward construction (16%), retail (16%), services (21%) and manufacturing (14%). No AI-by-industry table is published. — [NFIB 2025 Technology Survey PDF](https://www.nfib.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-NFIB-Technology-Survey.pdf)

**Federal Reserve Small Business Credit Survey 2025 (6,525 employer firms with 1-499 employees; convenience sample; Sept 3-Nov 14, 2025)**
- 46% of firms say the business or its employees use AI; 15% plan to within 12 months; 33% have no plans.
- Among users, about half are experimenting, 44% have partially integrated AI, and 7% have fully integrated it.
- The public report has no industry breakdown of AI use. — [Fed SBCS 2026 Report on Employer Firms](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2026/2026-report-on-employer-firms)
- In the 2024 SBCS, nearly 40% were using or planning to use AI. — [SF Fed brief](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/community-development-research-briefs/2026/07/ai-adoption-in-small-businesses-2024-sbcs/) (search snippet only; not fetched)

**Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses Voices (program participants, so likely skews toward growth-oriented firms)**
- Jan 27-Feb 4, 2026 wave (n=1,256): 76% use AI; only 14% have fully integrated AI into core operations. An earlier 2025 wave found 68%. — reported via [Fox Business (2025 wave)](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/small-business-ai-adoption-jumps-68-owners-plan-significant-workforce-growth-2025) and [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/goldman-small-businesses-using-ai-say-it-has-a-positive-impact--and-not-because-it-is-replacing-workers-130054910.html?amp=&amp=). The Goldman primary page returned 403, so these figures are second-hand.

**Home services / trades (vendor surveys of their own customers or panels)**
- Housecall Pro 2026 ("AI in the Trades," 248 US home-service pros, May-June 2026, mostly its own customers): 48% actively use AI. Electrical 54%, plumbing 53%. The 2025 wave (n=420) found 70%+ had tried AI and about 40% were active users. — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/ai-in-the-trades/); [PR Newswire 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/70-of-home-service-professionals-now-use-ai-to-cut-admin-work-not-field-jobs-housecall-pro-report-finds-302468294.html)
- ServiceTitan (about 1,000 residential contractors): about 25% current use; only 12% have AI embedded in daily operations and 34% are experimenting. — search-result summary only; primary report not fetched
- Jobber (Dec 2025, 1,050 home-service owners): 52% using AI, with HVAC 81.5%, roofing 67%, plumbing 65% and electrical 64%. — **unverified secondary write-up**; Jobber's primary report was not accessed

**Restaurants**
- Vendor-sponsored restaurant surveys report very high "use" figures, such as 95% of 600 independent full-service operators, with phone answering at 29% of uses. These come from search snippets with unclear dates and are not reliable for citation. They conflict with BTOS, which puts Accommodation & Food Services at 8% (end 2025). [Restaurant Business Online](https://restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/even-mom-pops-are-investing-artificial-intelligence)

### Inferences
- Across every rigorous source, the lowest-adoption small-business sectors are Construction, Transportation/Warehousing, Agriculture, and Accommodation & Food Services, each under 10% on the BTOS and JPMC measures. Retail (about 14%) and Manufacturing (about 12%) are next. Census authors also flag Other Services (salons, dry cleaners, auto repair) as low-use, though no current number was published. These sectors together account for more than 6 million firms ([Census CES-WP-24-16](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/CES-WP-24-16.pdf)).
- The highest-adoption sectors are Information, Professional/Scientific/Technical Services (legal, accounting, consulting, marketing), Education, Finance/Insurance, and Real Estate at roughly 24-40%. These are the most contested markets for an AI agency.
- Vendor surveys of trades (Housecall Pro, Jobber) show 40-50%+ "use," but their samples are software-using customers. The general population of trades firms is far lower, near 9% by the JPMC paid-tool measure. Most local trades firms are therefore still untouched.
- Restaurants show the widest gap between definitions. Owners embedded in POS ecosystems (Toast and similar) say they use AI, but BTOS firm-level use is only 8%. This suggests AI arrives bundled in software rather than as a deliberate deployment.
- Micro firms (1-4 employees) show the flattest expected growth (18% to 21%), which signals low internal momentum. Uptake there will likely depend on done-for-you providers.

### Gaps
- No published Census number for Other Services (NAICS 81: salons, auto repair, dry cleaners) or Health Care for small firms only. The BTOS sector figure (Figure 2 of CES-WP-26-25) is an image and could not be extracted. Exact current values for Construction, Agriculture, Transportation and Accommodation/Food under the new question are known only as "<10%" (Minneapolis Fed) and 8% (Accommodation/Food, Fed Board).
- BTOS sector figures cover firms of all sizes, although small firms are about 90% of responses (Fed note). I found no published size-by-sector crosstab.
- Jobber and ServiceTitan figures could not be verified from primary sources.
- No reliable salon/beauty, dental, auto repair, or landscaping-specific AI adoption survey was found.

## 2. Lowest vs. highest sectors, with numbers and change 2023-2026

### Takeaway
The ranking has been stable since BTOS began in September 2023: Information on top, and Construction and Agriculture at the bottom. Levels roughly tripled to quadrupled on every measure from 2023 to 2025. The Census question change in Nov 2025 broke the series, so pre- and post-revision levels cannot be compared directly.

### Cited Findings
- **Sept 2023 to Feb 2024 (old BTOS question, "AI in producing goods or services"):** overall use rose from 3.7% to 5.4%. By sector, use ranged from **1.4% in Construction and Agriculture** to **18.1% in Information**. Expected use ranged from 1.5% in Agriculture to 21.5% in Information. Employment-weighted, Construction and Mining were lowest. The supplement sample was about 164,500 businesses. — [Census CES-WP-24-16 (2024)](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/CES-WP-24-16.pdf)
- The 2024 paper says: "a large fraction of firms in the U.S. economy (more than 6 million firms) are small firms in Construction, Other Services (e.g., drycleaners, hair and nail salons) and Accommodation and Food Services. Our findings highlight low usage rates in these sectors." — [Census CES-WP-24-16](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/CES-WP-24-16.pdf)
- **Pre-revision growth by size:** 1-4 employee firms went from about 4% to about 10%, and 250+ firms from about 5% to about 13%, between Sept 2023 and late 2025. After the revision, firms with <20 employees are at about 18% and 250+ firms at about 31%. — [Census CES-WP-26-25](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf)
- **Late 2025 (old question):** about 10% of businesses, double the mid-2024 rate. **Early 2026 (new question):** about 20%. — [Minneapolis Fed](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2026/ai-adoption-in-business-grows-steadily-but-unevenly)
- The old question's adoption grew 68% (3.9 points) year over year before slowing in Q2 2025. — [Fed FEDS Note](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html)
- **JPMC trajectory** (consistent definition): 8.3% at end 2023, 11.6% at end 2024, 17.7% at end 2025. Over 2019-2025, Construction rose from about 0% to 8.9%, Transportation from about 0% to 5.4%, and Information from about 5% to 39.3%. — [JPMC Institute](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/business-growth-and-entrepreneurship/understanding-ai-use-by-small-businesses)
- **US Chamber:** 23% (2023), about 40% (2024), 58% (2025). — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/08/18/us-chamber-report-small-businesses-rapidly-adopting-ai-despite-regulatory-concerns/)
- **Thryv:** 39% (2024) to 55% (2025). — [Thryv PDF](https://assets.thryv.com/prod/media/pdfs/guides/AI-and-Small-Business-Adoption-2025-FINAL.pdf)
- **Housecall Pro (trades):** about 40% active (2025) to 48% active (2026). The samples differ and are not directly comparable. — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/ai-in-the-trades/)
- **Conflicting data:** a NEXT Insurance survey of 1,500 small business owners (April 2025) reported AI use falling from 42% (2024) to 28% (2025). — [AOL/Yahoo coverage](https://www.aol.com/finance/small-business-ai-adoption-declines-153004044.html) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- The gap between sectors has widened in absolute points. In 2023-24 it was 1.4% vs. 18.1% (about 17 points). By 2025 it was about 8.9% vs. 39% on the JPMC measure (about 30 points). Low-adoption sectors are growing but falling further behind.
- Suggested tiers for the report:
  - **Lowest:** Construction, Transportation, Agriculture, Accommodation/Food, Other Services
  - **Low-middle:** Retail, Manufacturing, Wholesale
  - **Middle:** Health care (about 20%), Real Estate (24%)
  - **Highest:** Information, Professional Services, Education, Finance

### Gaps
- No consistent small-firm-only sector series covers 2023 to 2026. The BTOS series broke in Nov 2025.

## 3. Barriers cited by low-adoption sectors

### Takeaway
The dominant barrier in the representative data is perception: "AI is not applicable to my business," cited by 65-81% of non-adopters. Knowledge gaps and privacy concerns come far behind, and cost is a minor factor in Census data. Small-business surveys add unfamiliarity (42% not at all familiar, per NFIB), time to implement, and difficulty finding tools that fit.

### Cited Findings
- **BTOS 2026 supplement** (firms not planning near-term AI use): "AI not applicable to this business" 65%; lack of knowledge of AI capabilities 22%; privacy/security concerns 20%. Using vendors/consultants and laws/regulations were the least cited. — [Census CES-WP-26-25](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf)
- **BTOS 2023-24 supplement:** not applicable 80.9%, lack of knowledge 7.3%, privacy/security 6.6%, technology not mature 6.1%, **too expensive 4.1%**, lack of skilled workforce 2.9%, lack of data 2.2%. The authors note that "some firms lacking such knowledge may have responded as AI being not applicable." — [Census CES-WP-24-16](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/CES-WP-24-16.pdf)
- **Persistence:** 71.5% of current non-users plan to remain non-users over the next 6 months, and only 9.4% plan to switch. — [Census CES-WP-26-25](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf)
- **Fed SBCS 2025:** over half of firms with no plans say AI is not applicable, and 30% prefer not to use it. Planned users struggle to find tools that meet their needs (54%) and with the time to implement or train staff (37%). Current users cite accuracy (46%) and adapting tools to business needs (43%). — [Fed SBCS](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2026/2026-report-on-employer-firms)
- **NFIB 2025:** 42% are not at all familiar with AI and 46% don't follow AI developments in their industry. Among firms that haven't adopted any new technology, 25% say nothing on the market would meaningfully improve their business and 20% say it is too expensive. — [NFIB PDF](https://www.nfib.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-NFIB-Technology-Survey.pdf)
- **Housecall Pro 2026 (trades):** 52% of non-users are not familiar with AI tools built for the trades; a third plan to try AI but haven't yet. — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/ai-in-the-trades/)
- **Thryv 2025:** blue-collar owners are less optimistic about AI (about 56% more optimistic vs. 71-72% for services and white-collar). — [Thryv PDF](https://assets.thryv.com/prod/media/pdfs/guides/AI-and-Small-Business-Adoption-2025-FINAL.pdf)
- **Goldman 10KSB:** many users have not integrated AI deeply (14% full integration), and coverage emphasizes a need for training and support. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/goldman-small-businesses-using-ai-say-it-has-a-positive-impact--and-not-because-it-is-replacing-workers-130054910.html?amp=&amp=)
- **US Chamber 2025:** 65% worry about changing tech regulation and 65% about state-law compliance. — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/08/18/us-chamber-report-small-businesses-rapidly-adopting-ai-despite-regulatory-concerns/)

### Inferences
- In low-adoption sectors the main barrier is perceived relevance and awareness, not price. A plumber or restaurant owner does not see that AI could answer calls or book jobs. This suggests selling outcomes ("never miss a call," "more 5-star reviews") rather than selling "AI."
- Cost appears minor in Census data, but small-business surveys (NFIB) still cite expense for technology generally. Low monthly pricing with clear ROI is probably still needed.

### Gaps
- No source breaks down barriers by sector, so the barrier shares above apply to all non-adopters.

## 4. Which AI use cases small businesses actually use

### Takeaway
Small-business AI use is dominated by writing, marketing and content. Customer service is a minority use in general SMB surveys (9% in NFIB; well behind marketing in Census). Trades are an exception: customer communication/follow-up (52%) and quoting (51%) lead among Housecall Pro AI users. Customer-facing automation such as AI receptionists, booking, lead follow-up and review requests remains thinly adopted, which is the agency's opening.

### Cited Findings
- **Census BTOS 2026, among AI-using firms:** Sales & Marketing 52%, Strategy/Business Development 45%, IT 41%, R&D 40%. Customer service, finance/accounting and service provision are in the next tier. The largest expected increases are in sales & marketing, strategy, **customer service**, and finance/accounting. 57% of users use AI in three or fewer functions. Generative-AI tasks are led by writing, document analysis and information search. — [Census CES-WP-26-25](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-25.pdf)
- **Fed SBCS 2025, among users:** writing/marketing 83%, individual productivity 61%, planning/analysis 51%. — [Fed SBCS](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2026/2026-report-on-employer-firms)
- **NFIB 2025, among users (n=118), using or planning to use AI for:** communications (email, memos, documents) 29%, marketing/advertising 27%, business analysis 14%, **customer service 9%**, accounting 4%, process automation 4%. 44% of users say AI arrived embedded in products they already use rather than being deliberately introduced. — [NFIB PDF](https://www.nfib.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-NFIB-Technology-Survey.pdf)
- **Thryv 2025:**
  - Areas incorporating AI: business automation 49%, social media 49%, data analysis 48%, content creation 48%, customer relations 47%, marketing campaigns 46%, sales 34%.
  - Tool types: data analysis 62%, content generators 55%, marketing automations 47%, **customer chatbots 46%**, AI agents 30%.
  - Expected benefits: 66% agree AI can benefit them by automatically responding to customer reviews. 80% say AI is necessary to reach new customers and 78% say it is needed to keep up with customer expectations for fast response. — [Thryv PDF](https://assets.thryv.com/prod/media/pdfs/guides/AI-and-Small-Business-Adoption-2025-FINAL.pdf)
- **US Chamber 2025:** marketing/promotions 46%, payroll 44%, CRM 42%. 63% rely mainly on externally developed tools. — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/08/18/us-chamber-report-small-businesses-rapidly-adopting-ai-despite-regulatory-concerns/)
- **Housecall Pro 2026 (trades AI users):** customer communication/follow-up 52%, estimates/quoting/pricing 51%, business planning/reporting 43%. 40% say AI helps them respond faster and lose fewer leads. In one case study, the share of callers leaving voicemail rose from 15-20% to 60-70% after an AI answering service (anecdote). In the 2025 homeowner survey, 53% were comfortable with AI handling the initial inquiry. — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/ai-in-the-trades/)
- **Restaurants:** a vendor survey snippet put phone answering at 29% of AI uses among independent full-service restaurants. Date and sample were unverified. — [Restaurant Business Online](https://restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/even-mom-pops-are-investing-artificial-intelligence)

### Inferences
- Most current small-business AI use is internal and self-serve: ChatGPT for writing, Canva and social posts. Done-for-you customer-facing systems are much less common: AI receptionist, automated booking, speed-to-lead follow-up, quote generation, and review-request automation. Customer service appears at 9% (NFIB), and in Census it ranks behind marketing and strategy.
- Census shows customer service among the largest expected increases, and Thryv shows strong owner belief that fast response is required (78%). Demand is forming before supply has reached low-adoption sectors.
- Trades firms that adopt AI go straight to customer communication and quoting. This validates those use cases for construction-adjacent trades, the largest low-adoption pool.

### Gaps
- No representative (Census-quality) data on AI receptionist/phone-answering, online booking, or review-request automation adoption by sector.
- No salon, dental, auto repair, landscaping, cleaning, or pet-services specific adoption data was found.
- Salesforce SMB Trends and Intuit QuickBooks AI survey data were not retrieved within the time budget.
