# Experience Niches: Party/Event Rentals and Construction Subcontractors (plus adjacent niches) for a small US web/AI agency

Research date: 2026-10-08. US focus. Note: IBISWorld free pages show figures from different publication vintages (2024, 2025, 2026 editions) that often conflict; conflicts are flagged. Many statistics on missed calls, AOV and AI adoption come from vendors with a commercial interest and are labeled as such.

## Q1. Party/event rentals: market size, growth, businesses, tickets, seasonality, online booking, existing tools and AI

### Takeaway
US party/event rental is a mid-size (~$5.5-8.5B depending on definition), fragmented market of roughly 7,400-9,800 businesses (IBISWorld) with ARA forecasting strong growth in 2026 (+9.5%). Tickets are small for inflatables ($150-$500) but larger for tents/tables/wedding packages (~$2,000 avg wedding rental spend). Demand is heavily seasonal (spring/summer, weekends), so peak-season inquiry overload is real. Vertical software (InflatableOffice, Goodshuffle Pro, Booqable) already handles online booking/quotes/payments; native AI is only just appearing (Booqable "Bo," July 2026, which is back-office, not customer-facing), leaving room for agency-built customer-facing AI chat/voice/follow-up.

### Cited Findings
**Market size and growth**

Definition: rental of tables, chairs, flatware, linens, centerpieces, stages, tents, canopies for public and private events. — [IBISWorld market size](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/party-supply-rental/4389); [IBISWorld industry stats](https://ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/market-size/party-supply-rental-united-states)
- A newer IBISWorld page gives 2026 market $8.5B, with a projected 0.6% decline in 2026 alone (conflicts with other year-by-year figures; check the current edition). — [IBISWorld industry report](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/party-supply-rental/4389/)
- Business count: 7,392 businesses in 2025 (declined at -0.6% CAGR 2020-2025) per one IBISWorld page; 9,849 businesses in 2026 (grew at 3.8% CAGR 2021-2026) per a newer page. These conflict, likely due to methodology revision. — [IBISWorld number of businesses](https://script.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/party-supply-rental/4389); [IBISWorld industry stats](https://www.ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/number-of-businesses/party-supply-rental-united-states/)
- IBISWorld lists Party Rental Ltd. with a 36.8% share (2024) in its company table — this seems high for a fragmented industry and should be treated with caution. — [IBISWorld industry report](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/party-supply-rental/4389/)
- American Rental Association (forecasts produced by S&P Global): US party & event rental grew 10.8% in 2024 to $5.3B (Dec 2024 release); May 2025 forecast 2.8% growth in 2025 to $5.4B; Aug 2025 forecast $5.5B in 2025 (+3.8%) and $5.7B in 2026; latest quarterly forecast: +9.5% in 2026 to $6.2B, then +8.3% in 2027 and +6.4% in 2028. — [Rental Equipment Register: ARA latest quarterly forecast](https://www.rermag.com/news-analysis/headline-news/article/55400064/aras-latest-quarterly-forecast-projects-continuing-growth-through-2028); [RER: ARA updated equipment, event forecasts](https://www.rermag.com/news-analysis/headline-news/article/55379161/american-rental-association-releases-updated-equipment-event-economic-forecasts-for-north-america); [Rental Management (ARA news)](https://news.ararental.org/?p=12070)
- ARA ($5-6B) and IBISWorld ($8.5B) figures differ because of scope/definitions; neither should be added.

- Vendor claim: birthday parties are 60-70% of inflatable bookings, with customers spending $150-$300 per event (rental software vendor; unverified). — [Reservety bounce house business plan guide](https://reservety.com/guides/bounce-house/bounce-house-business-plan.html)

- Wedding-scale event rentals: couples spent ~$2,000 on event rentals on average (attributed to The Knot Real Weddings Study via secondary source; 2024 weddings). — [Complete Weddings + Events](https://completewedo.com/blog/what-are-real-couples-spending-on-weddings/)
- A small bounce house business listed for sale advertised ~$100K revenue (single listing, anecdotal). — [BizQuest listing](https://images.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/profitable-bounce-house-biz-100k-revenue/BW2402583)

**Seasonality**
- Peak demand in spring/summer warm-weather months, with higher peak pricing (low-quality content source). — [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/cost-to-rent-a-bounce-house/)

**Online booking**
- Vendor claim that ~46% of bounce house distribution runs through online channels (attributed to a market research firm; could not verify). — [Reservety bounce house software](https://reservety.com/solutions/bounce-house-rental-software.html)

**Existing tools and AI**
- InflatableOffice: inventory, quotes, contracts, order tracking, payments, customer records, POS, online booking (customers can self-book), Google Calendar/QuickBooks integrations; website included; pricing conflicting ($39/mo hosting per GetApp vs $85/mo starting per Software Advice). No AI features found. — [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/rental/inflatableoffice-profile/); [GetApp](https://www.getapp.co.uk/software/2060138/inflatableoffice); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/135628/inflatableoffice)
- Goodshuffle Pro: barcode inventory, real-time conflict detection, photo-rich proposals, e-signature and payments, delivery management, dashboards, online booking; integrates Google Calendar, Maps, QuickBooks, Stripe; pricing listed $39-$99/mo (conflicting). No AI features found. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/167364/Goodshuffle-Pro/); [FitGap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/goodshuffle-pro)
- Booqable launched "Bo," an AI assistant (July 30, 2026) that gives briefings on shortages, pickup/return conflicts, quotes needing follow-up, and answers revenue/product questions — an internal/operator assistant, not a customer-facing booking chatbot. — [Booqable What's New](https://booqable.com/whats-new/july_30_2026/)
- Third parties already sell AI chat/agents on top of Booqable (Tars, Runbear, Arahi) and an AI consultancy (Stormy) markets "AI front desk" specifically to party rental companies using InflatableOffice, Goodshuffle and Booqable — evidence of early agency/tool entry into this niche. — [Tars](https://hellotars.com/tools/booqable); [Runbear](https://runbear.io/use-cases-software/booqable); [Arahi](https://arahi.ai/ai-agent/booqable/chat-support); [Stormy party rental / InflatableOffice](https://stormy.ai/consult/party-rental/front-desk/inflatableoffice); [Stormy / Goodshuffle](https://stormy.ai/consult/party-rental/front-desk/goodshuffle)

### Inferences
- Pain points AI can solve: after-hours/weekend inquiries (parents book in evenings), "is this unit available on date X / does it fit my yard / power requirements / weather policy" FAQs, quote follow-up, abandoned online-cart recovery, review requests post-event, and surge handling in April-June. Rental software covers booking mechanics, so the agency play is a layer on top (AI chat/voice wired to InflatableOffice/Goodshuffle/Booqable availability, SMS follow-up, review automation, SEO/local sites).
- Low ticket for inflatable-only operators (~$150-$300) and small revenue (~$100K-type businesses) limits what they can pay; tent/table/wedding-focused rental companies (larger orders, quote-driven, longer sales cycles) are better retainer prospects.
- Saturation: low in general marketing-agency terms vs. roofing/HVAC, but nascent AI vendors (Stormy, Booqable add-ons) are appearing; Booqable's in-product AI may expand to customer-facing chat over time.

### Gaps

- No verified industry AOV benchmark; no ARA survey on party-rental software/AI adoption found.
- Event Rental Systems and Rentopian features/AI status not found in searches.
- Could not verify the IBISWorld Party Rental Ltd. 36.8% share claim.

## Q2. Construction subcontractors (drywall, metal framing, acoustical ceilings): counts, project values, how they win work, labor shortage, estimating tools/AI, pain points

### Takeaway
Drywall/insulation is a large, fragmented trade (IBISWorld: ~127K businesses, ~$115-122B revenue in 2025; narrower Census-style establishment counts ~18-19.5K employer firms). Commercial wall-and-ceiling subs win work mainly via GC bid invitations (BuildingConnected network >1M professionals), so the "inbound lead" problem is really bid-invitation triage and fast, accurate estimating. Labor shortages are severe (92% of firms struggle to hire; >90% of AWCI members cite craft and field-management shortages). AI takeoff (Togal.AI, Kreo) is targeting drywall specifically, but broad AI adoption among contractors remains low (roughly 18-35% using AI; <15% usage in 20 of 23 functions per Dodge/CMiC).

### Cited Findings
**Number of businesses and market size**
- IBISWorld Drywall & Insulation Installers (NAICS 23831): 127,366 businesses in 2025 (+1.1% vs 2024; -0.9%/yr avg 2020-2025); last updated Oct 2025. Earlier April 2025 edition listed ~129K. — [IBISWorld number of businesses](https://img3.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/drywall-insulation-installers/192); [IBISWorld industry](https://img3.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/drywall-insulation-installers/192)
- Revenue estimates conflict: IBISWorld ~$122.2B (2025) (April 2025 edition: $114.8B); First Research ~18,000 establishments with ~$45B revenue; Grata TAM ~$57B; VerticalIQ 19,495 drywall and insulation contractors. Differences reflect scope (IBISWorld counts non-employer/sole proprietors; establishment counts are employer firms). — [IBISWorld](https://img2.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-research-reports/drywall-insulation-installers-industry); [Grata](https://grata.com/market-research/238310-drywall-insulation-contractors); [VerticalIQ](https://verticaliq.com/?p=3588); [BVResources / First Research](https://www.bvresources.com/articles/aid/90189)
- NAICS 238310 covers drywall, plaster, building insulation and firestop services (acoustical ceiling installation is typically classified here as well). — [HigherGov NAICS 238310](https://www.highergov.com/naics/238310-drywall-and-insulation-contractors/)
- IBISWorld Insulation Contractors (separate): 28,814 businesses in 2025 (+1.4%; +1.6%/yr 2020-2025). — [IBISWorld Insulation Contractors](https://script.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/insulation-contractors/4870)

**How they win work**
- BuildingConnected (Autodesk) auto-sends invitations to bid when a GC publishes a project/bid package; subs manage invites in "Bid Board Pro," and "Bid Forwarding" pulls in invitations received outside BuildingConnected. GCs search subcontractors curated by scope, location and past engagement. — [Autodesk blog: Streamline bid management](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/construction/streamline-bid-management/); [BuildingConnected support](https://support.buildingconnected.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360022100314-How-do-tender-invitations-look-when-they-are-sent-from-BuildingConnected)
- BuildingConnected Pro network described as >1 million construction professionals (G2 listing; not an official Autodesk count). — [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/buildingconnected-pro)
- AWCI 2025 Trends Report (with FMI): contractors focused on increasing market share / serving current customers (i.e., GC relationships). — [Walls & Ceilings](https://www.wconline.com/articles/97837-awci-unveils-wall-and-ceiling-industry-trends-report)

**Market outlook and labor**
- AWCI 2025 Trends Report: >70% of members expect economy/construction market flat or declining; data centers and healthcare the main growth drivers through 2026; >90% cite shortages of both craftspeople and field management; product demand fell short of 2024 projections. — [Walls & Ceilings, Nov 2025](https://www.wconline.com/articles/97837-awci-unveils-wall-and-ceiling-industry-trends-report)
- AWCI 2024 report: 46% of contractors expected labor shortage to stay the same; only 28% expected improvement. — [ASA Online](https://www.asaonline.com/2024/10/29/awci-releases-wall-and-ceiling-industry-trends-report/); [BuildSteel](https://buildsteel.org/reports/awci-releases-wall-and-ceiling-industry-trends-report/)
- AGC/NCCER 2025 Workforce Survey (~1,400 firms, released Aug 28, 2025): 92% report difficulty filling positions; 88% have openings; 45% report project delays due to worker shortages; carpenters among hard-to-fill roles (~71-77%), superintendents 81%. — [NCCER infographic](https://www.nccer.org/media/2025/10/AGC-NCCER_Workforce-Survey_infographic2025-Final.pdf); [NCCER](https://www.nccer.org/?p=22118); [Mondaq summary](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/construction-planning/1677264/agc-2025-workforce-shortages-causing-project-delays)

**Estimating tools and AI**
- Togal.AI: cloud AI takeoff measuring partitions and ceilings from drawings; suggests wall classifications for estimator review; launched "Assemblies" for drywall contractors (materials, labor, equipment per assembly using contractor's own formulas/costs/production rates) — drywall is its first targeted trade. Vendor claims 140-230 hours saved per project (Coastal Construction, 2025; vendor-reported). — [Togal drywall takeoff](https://www.togal.ai/trade/drywall-takeoff-software); [Togal news: drywall assemblies](https://www.togal.ai/news/drywall-estimating-assemblies); [Togal blog](https://www.togal.ai/blog/drywall-assemblies)
- Kreo: computer-vision plan scanning, ~$35/month entry pricing (third-party roundup). — [Dan Cumberland Labs roundup](https://dancumberlandlabs.com/blog/ai-construction-estimating-software/)
- Capterra's Togal listing showed zero reviews at time of search — weak signal of limited sub-level adoption. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10001876/ScopeTakeoff/)

**AI adoption among contractors (2025)**
- Houzz 2025 US State of AI in Construction & Design: >1 in 3 construction professionals using AI; large firms ~2x the rate. — [BD+C](https://bdcnetwork.com/home/news/55307457/more-than-one-third-of-construction-professionals-are-using-ai)
- Dodge Construction Network/CMiC (235 US contractors, fall 2025): 87% expect AI to improve project delivery, but usage below 15% in 20 of 23 functions; 57% concerned about data accuracy, 54% data security. — [Roads & Bridges](https://roadsbridges.com/technology/news/55337001/report-most-contractors-expect-ai-to-change-how-projects-get-built)
- Another North American survey (150 respondents, 2025): only 18% report active daily use of AI tools. — [Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/builders-ai-survey-adoption-gap-construction/761632/)
- RICS (global, 2,200+): 45% of organizations no AI use; ~1% scaled across projects. — [Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/builders-ai-survey-adoption-gap-construction/761632/)
- ServiceTitan trades survey (1,032 contractors, Oct-Nov 2025): 60% familiar with AI (self-described as non-representative). — [ServiceTitan AI report PDF](https://get.servicetitan.com/rs/304-EAE-038/images/0102038_ST_AI_Report%20v2-4.pdf?version=0)

### Inferences
- For commercial drywall/framing/ACT subs, the high-value AI use cases are: bid-invitation triage (parse ITBs from BuildingConnected/email, score fit, summarize scope/specs/addenda), scope-letter/exclusion drafting, takeoff assistance integrated with existing tools (Togal/STACK/PlanSwift/Bluebeam), bid-leveling of supplier quotes, bid follow-up/CRM with GCs, and recruiting/hiring funnels (given 90%+ labor shortage). A website/AI receptionist is lower value since inbound demand is relationship/bid-driven, not consumer search.
- Ticket sizes (commercial projects often $50K-$millions per subcontract) make even small efficiency gains valuable, but sales cycles are slow and owners are conservative (data accuracy/security concerns).
- The Chicago client provides a credible case study for selling "AI bid desk" services to other AWCI-type subs — a niche few marketing agencies serve, unlike roofing.

- No source found for typical drywall subcontract values or bid hit rates (commonly cited anecdotally as 10-25% win rates but not verified here).
- No data found on STACK/PlanSwift market share or AI features, or on percentage of drywall subs using AI takeoff.
- ABC-specific 2025/2026 labor shortage figure (e.g., workers needed) not retrieved.

## Q3. Adjacent niches: comparative economics and which offer the best mix of high ticket, inbound demand and low AI adoption

### Takeaway
Among event-side neighbours, wedding venues (avg ~$12,200 spend) and full-service tent/event rental companies combine high tickets with heavy inbound inquiry volume; DJs (~$1,700) and photographers (~$2,900) have mid tickets but are numerous and heavily served by marketplaces (The Knot, WeddingWire). Among construction neighbours, roofing (~106K businesses, $76-92B) and remodeling ($174B) have high tickets and strong inbound consumer demand but are the most saturated with marketing agencies and AI receptionist vendors; commercial specialty subs (drywall, ACT, insulation) have low agency saturation but bid-driven rather than inbound demand. Best candidate mixes: (1) tent/event rental and wedding venues on side A; (2) residential remodelers/insulation/flooring and commercial specialty subs (bid desk AI) on side B, avoiding a head-on fight in roofing.

### Cited Findings
**Event-side neighbours**
- Wedding costs (The Knot study, 2024 weddings, via secondary source): avg ~$33,000 total; venue $12,200; photographer $2,900; caterer ~$80/guest; DJ $1,700; florist $2,700; event rentals $2,000. 2025 weddings avg ~$34,000 (newer study; breakdown not found). Median per SoFi ~$10,000. — [Complete Weddings + Events](https://completewedo.com/blog/what-are-real-couples-spending-on-weddings/); [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/wedding-season-here-how-much-one-cost-2025)
- Wedding planners (IBISWorld): 21,714 businesses in 2025 (4.3% CAGR 2020-2025), ~$1.5B revenue; DIY planning pressures growth. — [IBISWorld wedding planners](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/wedding-planners/4412/)
- Party & event planners (IBISWorld): 3,449 businesses, $1.7B (2025, Aug 2025 edition); a July 2026 edition revises to $2.9B and 3,379 businesses; a Sept 2024 profile listed 94,293 businesses and $6.9B (different scope). Weddings are the largest segment. — [IBISWorld party & event planners](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/party-event-planners/4414/); [IBISWorld number of businesses](https://ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/number-of-businesses/party-event-planners-united-states)

**Construction-side neighbours**
- Roofing contractors: 105,876 businesses in 2025 (+2.7%), 227,359 employees; revenue $76.4B (2025, one edition) vs $92.5B (2026, another edition); growth tied to severe weather. — [IBISWorld roofing](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/roofing-contractors/198/); [IBISWorld roofing businesses](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/roofing-contractors/198)
- Remodeling: $174.3B in 2025 (+1.7%), $175.4B in 2026. — [IBISWorld remodeling](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/remodeling/2013)
- Painting (NAICS 23832): ~$44.0B (2025), ~230K businesses declining 0.7%/yr; house painting & decorating: 223,209 businesses (2025), ~$28.4B (2026). — [IBISWorld painters](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/painters-in-the-us/187/); [IBISWorld house painting](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/house-painting-decorating-contractors/5738/)
- Flooring installers: $33.8B (2026), ~110K businesses (declined 1.3%/yr 2020-2025). — [IBISWorld flooring](https://img3.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/flooring-installers/196)
- Insulation contractors: 28,814 businesses (2025), growing 1.6%/yr. — [IBISWorld insulation](https://script.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/insulation-contractors/4870)

**Saturation / agency and AI vendor evidence**
- Roofing has dedicated roofing-only marketing agencies and AI receptionist vendors (My AI Front Desk, Ainora, CI Web Group) actively marketing to roofers; one 2025 guide claims fewer than 30% of roofers use AI in marketing (unsourced). — [My AI Front Desk roofing](https://www.myaifrontdesk.com/blogs/ai-receptionist-for-roofing-companies); [Ainora](https://ainora.lt/blog/ai-receptionist-for-roofers-contractors-2026); [CI Web Group](https://www.ciwebgroup.com/blog/roofing-company-ai-marketing); [Fixr roofing leads](https://www.fixr.com/pros/roofing-leads)
- Missed-call benchmarks (useful for the AI receptionist pitch): CallRail Jan 2025 home services missed-call rate 14%; Invoca (July 2026) humans answered only 52% of inbound home-services calls (65% excluding calls <=15 sec). Higher figures (27%, 62%, 74%) are vendor marketing without verifiable methodology. — [JustCall missed call statistics](https://justcall.io/blog/missed-call-statistics.html); [Focus Digital speed-to-lead](https://focus-digital.co/average-speed-to-lead-by-industry-and-what-every-minute-of-delay-costs/); [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/home-services-missed-call-revenue-statistics-2026)

### Inferences
- Scoring (qualitative, based on above):
  - Wedding/event venues: high ticket ($12K avg), heavy inbound inquiries (tours, date availability, pricing), weekend/evening inquiry timing; moderate tooling (marketplaces, venue CRMs) — strong fit.
  - Tent/table/large event rental companies: $2K+ orders, quote-driven, seasonal surges, vertical software has little customer-facing AI — strong fit and closest to owner's experience.
  - Photo booths/DJs: mid ticket ($1-3K), many small operators, marketplace-dominated — moderate fit, lower willingness to pay.
  - Event planners/catering: event planners are a small segment (~3.4K businesses by IBISWorld's narrow definition); catering per-guest pricing yields high tickets — not deeply researched here.
  - Roofing: high ticket, strong inbound demand, but most saturated with agencies/AI receptionist vendors — avoid head-on or differentiate.
  - Remodelers/insulation/flooring/painters: high-ish tickets and inbound demand with less specialized-agency saturation than roofing; painters are numerous but many tiny.
  - Commercial drywall/framing/ACT subs: very high tickets, low agency saturation, low AI adoption, but bid-driven; best sold as operations/bid automation rather than marketing.
- No source directly measured agency saturation by niche; saturation judgments rely on observed density of niche-specific vendors in search results.

### Gaps
- No US market-size or business-count data found for photo booth rentals, DJs, or caterers in this round.
- No direct, independent measure of marketing-agency density by niche.
- No AI adoption data specific to event rental, wedding venues, or individual construction trades.
- Remodeler business count and concrete contractor data not retrieved.
